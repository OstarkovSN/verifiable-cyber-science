Question: A cup of coffee is poured at 90 °C in a room at 20 °C. Ten minutes later it is at 70 °C. Assuming Newton's law of cooling with a constant coefficient, when will it reach 50 °C? Give a guaranteed interval in minutes.

Measurements: At elapsed time 0 minutes, coffee temperature is exactly 90 °C; at 10 minutes, exactly 70 °C. Room temperature is exactly 20 °C.

Model assumptions: The room stays at 20 °C. The coffee has a single uniform temperature. Its temperature is a differentiable real function of elapsed minutes, with rate equal to minus a constant cooling coefficient times its temperature difference from the room. No other heating or cooling effects, changes in the coefficient, or measurement uncertainties are included. We select the exponential solution T(t) = 20 + 70 exp(-kt), with k = log(7/5)/10 per minute, calibrated to the exact measurements. The mathematical function is defined for all real times; the physical question concerns nonnegative elapsed times.

Lean will check directly that the selected function satisfies Newton's differential law, matches both measurements, and reaches exactly 50 °C at the defined time log(7/3)/k. It will check this time lies in the closed interval [25,26] minutes and is the unique nonnegative time when this function reaches 50 °C. Lean will not check the empirical validity of Newton's law, the measurements, uniformity of the coffee temperature, or experimental uncertainties. It does not prove a general differential-equation uniqueness theorem deriving this function from arbitrary solutions; the exponential solution is explicitly selected as the model.
