# VCS-LK-001: operator-only lake and survey simulator

**Keep this document, `lake.py`, and operator receipts outside scientist inputs.** The two researchers investigate one fixed fictional lake; only selected observation exports may later be shared with both. This implementation supplies no experiment response automatically and invokes no scientist client.

The scenario is an unnamed lake in the Lieksa region of North Karelia, eastern Finland. It has no real coordinates or real-lake identity. The design represents an irregular 6 ha lake within an approximately 300 × 250 m bounding box, with maximum depth 9 m. The three disjoint bottom-footprint habitat strata partition its surface area. This is an areal, stratified simulator: it does not construct a shoreline polygon, bathymetric grid or georeferenced fish trajectories.

The census covers **all living fish, including small juveniles**, throughout one closed two-day late-August window. Small fish are ≤8 cm; large fish are >8 cm. Population, stratum membership and size-bin counts never change between methods, passes or survey IDs. Exchangeable within-stratum encounters approximate movement; birth, death, migration, growth, capture and permanent removal are excluded. Two observation visits may see the same fish. The census is a fixed integer truth, not a randomly redrawn population distribution. Researchers may express uncertainty about it in their own way.

The tables below are designer-selected hidden truth, **not survey observations**. They are generated by `uv run python lake.py truth --format markdown` from the code specification; a test checks exact agreement. The environmental values and biological composition are invented scenario choices, not measurements of a Finnish lake or constants estimated from the references.

The eight-taxon **reference panel** includes perch, roach, ruffe, pike, burbot, bleak, vendace and common bream. It remains identical in every observation export and every relevant calibration, including zero-abundance taxa. Panel membership therefore cannot be used as a presence list. The five positive populations and their size/habitat allocation above are fixed modeling choices, not a claim that these taxa must coexist at those densities in any particular natural lake.

## Running and inspecting

This checkout already uses `uv` and Click; no new dependency is needed. Python 3.11 or newer is supported. For byte-for-byte replay retain the source and Python interpreter recorded in the receipt.

```sh
uv sync --group dev
uv run python lake.py truth
uv run python lake.py truth --format markdown
uv run python lake.py survey --method all --survey-id baseline --out artifacts/lake-example/export --operator-root artifacts/lake-example/operator
uv run python lake.py survey --method edna --survey-id followup --habitat littoral --habitat deep --sites 4 --out artifacts/lake-followup/export --operator-root artifacts/lake-followup/operator
uv run pytest tests/test_lake.py -q
```

The survey writes exactly `observations.json` inside the chosen export directory. Its common metadata labels the entire dataset **SYNTHETIC** once, gives the survey ID, reference panel, units/size bins, selected strata and effort. Each method has a design description and observed rows. Missing habitats are explicitly omitted in metadata; they are not zero counts. Zero-count reference taxa remain in every species-count/read mapping. The output includes no population answer, per-habitat population count, fish identity, hidden nuisance value, source hash or random seed.

The operator root must be a separate, non-nested tree. Each receipt directory there contains `lake.py`, `truth.json` and `receipt.json`: exact simulator source, hidden world, options, Python version, source/configuration and exported-file hashes. These files are not for either researcher. Existing export or receipt directories are refused. The export root itself contains no receipt or pointer to the hidden source. Later, an operator can supply the **same exact observation file** to both researchers rather than generate independent information for each. No delivery has been performed by this implementation.

`--method` accepts `habitat`, `edna`, `sonar`, `camera`, `calibration`, or `all`. `--habitat` can repeat; default is all three. `--sites` allows 1–6 stations per selected stratum. `--passes` allows 1–4 repeated sonar/camera passes; eDNA always uses two field replicates × three PCRs per station. Pass and station labels carry no persistent fish identity. More passes can increase information but cannot be added as different fish or unique spatial coverage.

The same survey ID and effective options produce identical observation bytes, independent of method call order or other requests. A method requested alone has the same rows as its section in an `all` bundle. Adding a habitat, station or pass leaves existing observation streams unchanged. New survey IDs change measurement/encounter noise only; they do not select a new lake. Stream keys derive from a private fixed identifier, version, survey ID and method/site/replicate/taxon labels. This is deterministic simulation, not cryptographic concealment: anyone given the source could inspect its truth. Filesystem separation is essential.

## Observation mechanisms and controls

**Habitat.** One noisy map-area estimate is drawn per selected stratum and reused by sonar/camera coverage reporting. Station soundings fluctuate around stratum means; water chemistry and temperatures have instrument/grab noise. Littoral/open water use the surface scenario and deep stations use the deep scenario. Secchi is omitted at shallow littoral stations rather than treating a bottom-limited reading as a transparency estimate. Exported area fractions use the noisy map estimates, not hidden true areas. Mapping reference polygons and depth fixtures provide finite independent instrument trials. Area/depth uncertainty therefore does not disappear merely by requesting another modality.

**eDNA.** Water samples are spatially stratified and station-labelled, with two separate 1 L field grabs/extractions per station and three nested PCR libraries each. The virtual 12S panel and nominal 0.45 µm filtration are assay metadata, not a validated real primer protocol. Each taxon's signal depends on stratum fish density per water volume, a mixture with lake-wide density, a fixed station contrast, taxon-specific shedding and a shared field/extraction multiplier. Shared field loss and individual PCR dropout create correlated nested observations. Amplification bias, PCR variation, finite sequencing and a background/unassigned category mean read fractions are not numerical fish fractions. Rare field and reagent contamination may generate reads from absent taxa. Two field blanks and two reagent blanks each have three PCRs; they are separate control samples, not zero-density lake stations. No blank is forcibly made positive or negative to fit a demonstration.

Operator details: signal blends 85% local and 15% whole-lake density, scaled per 1,000 m³; site contrasts cycle through 0.6, 0.8, 1.0 and 1.2. Taxon shedding and amplification coefficients live in `SHEDDING` and `AMPLIFICATION`. Field multipliers are mean-one lognormal (log SD 0.5), with 6% extraction loss; a taxon's field contamination chance is 1.2%. PCR multipliers have log SD 0.3; dropout probability is exp(−0.65 × signal), reagent contamination chance is 0.3% per taxon/reaction, and library sizes vary from 1,800 to 2,600. The background weight is 10. These are deliberately modest generative ingredients, not molecular kinetics, water circulation, DNA residence-time simulation, primer-sequence modeling, or a prescribed inference likelihood.

**Hydroacoustics.** Each stratum has repeated conceptual transect placements. Littoral transects are 100 m × 2 m, open transects 150 m × 6 m, and deep transects 100 m × 6 m. Hidden size-bin counts produce binomial encounters using true footprint fraction and habitat/size detection. Shore/vegetation, near-bottom availability and weak small-fish echoes are folded into these detection factors. Each size bin also has occasional non-fish target counts (Poisson mean 0.4 per strip). Exports contain only small/large target encounters, nominal footprint, measured-area coverage estimate and design details—**no species assignments**. Coarse size classification is assumed exact. This is neither acoustic wave propagation nor an echo-integration biomass estimator. A small-fish bin is included but is less detectable; the target population is never silently reduced to acoustically visible fish.

**Camera.** A ten-minute conceptual video footprint is 120 m² in littoral/deep habitat or 180 m² in open habitat. Binomial encounters use fixed population counts, footprint, habitat availability, taxon visibility and a 0.55 small-fish multiplier. Each taxon/size count also receives Poisson false labels with mean 0.015 per clip. Lighting is standardized and low-light; day/night fish redistribution is not modeled. Source constants expose the operator's detector values, while exports show only encounter counts and design. There is no persistent re-identification, actual camera reconstruction or detailed species-confusion matrix.

**Calibration.** Independent, known fixtures are explicitly labelled and cannot be mistaken for lake fish: single-taxon DNA standards at 0/2/20 copies, an equimolar eight-taxon DNA mixture, labelled reference video encounters, fish-free video controls per taxon and size bin, acoustic replay targets, fish-free acoustic strips and known-area/depth instrument fixtures. Relevant taxon calibrations always include all eight taxa. Trial counts and sampled successes/read counts are observations, not exact detection probabilities. PCR standards characterize detection/amplification, not natural shedding. Video fixtures omit field visibility/availability; acoustic reference detection is deliberately somewhat better than in the lake. Calibration-transfer uncertainty must remain visible. No lake fish are caught, handled, stocked or removed to calibrate a method.

## Limits and source motivation

This simulator is suitable for checking whether researchers distinguish species evidence, detection, survey coverage, calibration and total-population uncertainty. It has fixed strata, coarse lengths, exchangeable encounters, a closed census and a deliberately limited species universe. Uniform within-stratum encounter probabilities and simplified eDNA mixing omit real schooling, movements, oxygen-driven redistribution, seasonality, non-fish DNA transport and changing hydrology. Reference-panel absence is not proof that an unlisted species could not occur in nature. A bundle need not identify every taxon or tightly identify total abundance; the implementation does not manufacture an inverse-model solution or guarantee identifiability.

The following primary sources motivate the design; **none supplies this world's population counts or simulator constants**:

- [Turunen et al., *Ice fishing reveals size structure but not abundance of Eurasian perch in small boreal lakes*](https://doi.org/10.1111/fme.12603), Methods §2.1 and Table 1; [accepted full text](https://helda.helsinki.fi/server/api/core/bitstreams/43c8aa99-3bb6-42f9-8d45-e912df34eabe/content). The study describes eleven small boreal lakes near Lieksa and illustrates why a survey index need not directly measure abundance. Our simulator uses no angling or netting.
- [Skelton, Cauvin & Hunter, *Environmental DNA metabarcoding read numbers and their variability predict species abundance, but weakly in non-dominant species*](https://doi.org/10.1002/edn3.355), Methods §§2.1–2.3 and Results §3.2; [USGS record](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70237586). Replicated water sampling, blanks and an equimolar reference mixture motivate separate field/PCR controls and taxon bias; equal template input did not yield equal reads.
- [Kopf et al., *Combining Hydroacoustics and eDNA to Estimate Species-Specific Biomass in a Pelagic Fish Community*](https://doi.org/10.1002/edn3.70145), Methods §§2.1–2.6 and Results §§3.1–3.3. The paper discusses complementary modalities, acoustic exclusion zones and problematic deep eDNA accumulation. We model blind zones and transport effects schematically and do not copy its biomass-apportionment method or exclusions into this much shallower lake.
